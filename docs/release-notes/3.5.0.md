Physiscore 3.5.0 adds six analysis tools for charge transport, recombination and stability, opens a data file in the right tool by recognising what it holds, and collects the results of a whole project into one table. Everything is computed on your computer by documented rules and fits; nothing is sent over the network and no account is needed. The numbers of the existing tools are unchanged from 3.4.0.

### Six new tools
- **SCLC** - single-carrier J-V: ohmic, trap-filling and Child regions from the local slope, V_TFL and the trap density with its resolution limit, Mott-Gurney mobility, conductivity, sweep hysteresis.
- **Light intensity** - ideality factor from Voc against ln(irradiance) with its error, the Jsc power law, the Suns-Voc pseudo J-V curve, pseudo fill factor and the transport loss. Reads a table or a set of J-V files with the irradiance in their names.
- **TRPL** - one to three exponentials or a stretched exponential, chosen by AICc and weighted for counting statistics; amplitude- and intensity-weighted mean lifetimes, differential lifetime, diffusion length.
- **Impedance** - equivalent-circuit fit by complex least squares, circuit chosen by AICc among R(RC), R(RQ), R(RC)(RC), R(RQ)(RQ); errors of every parameter, effective capacitance, and a Kramers-Kronig test that says whether the spectrum can be fitted at all.
- **C-V (Mott-Schottky)** - the straight section of 1/C² is found automatically; doping density, built-in voltage, depletion width, doping profile, and the limit below which the result is the geometric capacitance rather than doping.
- **Stability** - T80 (measured, or extrapolated and marked as such), burn-in amplitude and time constant, stabilised Ts80, loss rate per 1000 h, with the ISOS protocol on record.

Each tool has a built-in example, takes several files at once, writes an Excel workbook with a side-car, lists its own validity checks and has an **Interpretation** with evidence, threshold, reference and next step. The equations and defaults are in the methods document, sections 17 to 22.

### Open data and the workspace
- **Open data...** (Ctrl+O): pick any measurement file; Physiscore reads the file name, the column headers and the numbers, decides which measurement it is and opens it in that tool. When the reading is uncertain it asks first.
- **Workspace folder...** (Ctrl+Shift+O): the Workspace tab lists every data file of a project folder with the measurement, the confidence and the reason. Open the selection in its tool, open it with another tool, or compare several files.
- **Results** tab: every export below the workspace folder with its key numbers; the list can be sorted and filtered by sample. **Collect results to Excel** writes them into one workbook (an index, one sheet per tool, one long table for pivoting).

### Graph tools in every window
- **Data reader** (Ctrl+R): click a curve to read the nearest measured point; a second click gives the differences and the slope between the two.
- **Plot details** (Ctrl+P): axis titles, limits and scales, and the name, colour, width and style of each curve.
- **Data worksheet** (Ctrl+D): the numbers behind every curve of the graph as a table that can be copied to Origin or Excel or saved as CSV or Excel.
- **Export figure** (Ctrl+Shift+S) saves at journal sizes: single column 85 mm, 1.5 column 114 mm, double column 174 mm, or a 16:9 slide.
- **Help → Methods of this tool** opens the offline methods document at the section for the window in use.

### Also
- Absorbance reads text exports (CSV, TSV, TXT, DAT, ASC) besides Excel.
- Command line: `sclc`, `intensity`, `trpl`, `eis`, `cv`, `stability`, `detect`, `collect`.
- 127 new tests and 24 new rows in the validation table.
