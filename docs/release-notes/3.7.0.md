Physiscore 3.7.0 adds two tools: **PLQY and QFLS**, which turns integrating-sphere spectra or a PLQY series into the quasi-Fermi-level splitting and the voltage losses of a film or cell, and **Spectral mismatch**, which computes the mismatch factor M of a solar simulator for each of your cells. The **Device summary** can now correct the J-V current by M. Every existing tool gives the same numbers as 3.6.0.

### New
- **PLQY and QFLS** (Transport and recombination). Load the three integrating-sphere spectra of a sample (empty sphere, sample out of the beam, sample in the beam) and get its absorptance and PLQY by the de Mello method; the laser and emission windows are found for you. Give the EQE of the same stack (or its band gap, or J0,rad) and the tool adds the quasi-Fermi-level splitting, the non-radiative loss -kT ln(PLQY) and, with your Voc, the voltage the contacts lose. A table of PLQY against excitation intensity adds the implied ideality factor and the implied J-V curve, FF and efficiency - the device without transport losses.
- **Spectral mismatch** (Device). Add the EQE files of your cells, choose the lamp spectrum and the reference cell calibration, and get M for each cell, the Jsc under AM1.5G and on the simulator, and the IEC 60904-9 spectral class of the lamp (2007 and 2020 editions). In the built-in example a xenon lamp of class A still makes a perovskite cell read 8.8 % too low against a silicon reference cell.
- **Device summary** has a *Spectral mismatch M* field: the J-V current is divided by M before it is compared with the EQE, and Jsc/M and PCE/M are listed.
- *Open data* and the Workspace tab recognise PLQY files.

### Changed
- File-path fields in the curve windows show the end of the path, so the file name is visible.

### Checks
- 13 new rows in the validation table (139 in all) and 59 new tests (583 in all).

### Known limitations
- The PLQY must come from spectra corrected for the response of the sphere and spectrometer; the tool cannot check that calibration.
- J0,rad from the band gap assumes a step absorptance and gives an upper estimate of the QFLS; use the EQE of the stack when you have it.
- The spectral class covers the spectral match only; the spectral coverage and deviation of the 2020 edition, and non-uniformity and temporal stability, are not computed.
